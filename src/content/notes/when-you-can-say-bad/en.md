For a long time I believed in universal feedback and used the “3 pluses” system. The whole team answers 3 questions:

- What did we do well?
- What could we do better?
- What should we add next time?

I was taught this at 15 in a debate club, and along with it I took on one more rule as a constant: saying “bad” is bad. Because such feedback leads nowhere and doesn’t move the work forward, it only stops it. But… hooray! How great that I’m not 15 anymore. The world turned out to be more complicated. In fact, saying “bad” is both allowed and not allowed.

<div class="ids__space M"></div>

## When you can’t say “bad”

- **As feedback to someone junior.** It simply doesn’t work. Feedback like that turns you into an aggressor with an intimidated team. Relationships in any team start with the “senior”, and in my view that’s the person most interested in growing the team and keeping up its enthusiasm. So they can’t just brush colleagues off and insult them. Their responsibility as a lead is to say specifically what can be done better. Step by step.
- **If your opinion is the final word.** If it’s final, then you were the one who set the task. So if the team did a “bad” job, most likely you set the task “badly”. Wake-up call, buddy!<br> <div class="ids__space S"></div> Instead of “bad”, here you need to go back to the start. Check how the task was set. Make sure the team understands it. Set expectations for the result. Renegotiate.
- **As a step to keep the work going.** Any evaluation — “bad”, “good”, “beautiful”, “clear”, “dumb” — gives nothing specific. After an evaluation it’s unclear what you’re being asked to do. Arguments, facts and advice are more useful than evaluations: they are specific and help you argue about the work itself.<br> <div class="ids__space S"></div> Most importantly, an evaluation affects the person. If a designer made a “bad” layout, it may seem that the designer is bad. Specifics don’t affect the person; they objectively show how to do better.<br> <div class="ids__space S"></div> Instead of “The layout is dumb and the font is wrong”, you need to put together separate steps. Think them through and describe them so that once they’re done, the layout really changes. For example: “The bulleted list has no indents. A display typeface doesn’t work for us, we need a grotesque”.

<div class="ids__space M"></div>

## When you can say “bad”

- **If your “bad” doesn’t affect anything.** You have no authority, so people have no reason to listen to you. You just want to speak your mind and can’t hold it in. For example, you, a designer, react to the developers’ work. Why do you need this? Unclear. Why do they need it? Even less clear. Oh well, let it be. That’s just the kind of person you are.
- **If you don’t know “So how should it be?”.** It means your sensations, feelings and intuition worked faster. Your brain hasn’t yet managed to formalize these abstractions, or can’t do it at all. In certain rare circumstances it’s acceptable to say: “This isn’t it. But I don’t understand why”. With these words you’ve given the team a dose of uncertainty, so it’s important to either come back with a formalized version: “Oh! I figured out what was wrong! First…”, or use the cancel card: “I still don’t get it, let’s keep going”.
- **If your boundaries have already been crossed.** React to situations that happen to you. When someone violates your sacred values, don’t start singing: <div class="ids__space S"></div> *— Oh, would you please share how it would be more mindful and comfortable for you to act, so that we reach a consensus and I can agree with you, without hurting your feelings, that you stop being late?* <div class="ids__space S"></div> <p>Your boundaries have already been crossed, and you need to say so. It will be better for the relationship to say:<br/>
<i>— I don’t like that you’re late. It doesn’t work for me.</i></p> <p>Life will become calmer, and people will be dearer.</p>

This article isn’t about a rainbow world where everyone without exception is a well-therapized, mindful bunny. It’s precisely because none of us are like that that it’s so important to rely on specifics and honesty at work. I believe this is the secret of fruitful teamwork.
