Because our brain is used to not doing it. Or isn’t used to doing it yet — depends on how you look at it.

Two habits of our brain are behind this, and every designer should know them.

<div class="ids__space M"></div>

## 1 — It’s easier for people to recognize than to recall

Anything that makes the brain switch on and dig something out is harder than choosing from what lies on the surface. The name is stored in memory; the face is right in front of us.

Good design doesn’t rely on a person’s memory or make them think or recall anything. It shows all the knowledge needed to make a decision.

It’s easier to answer “What do you want for dinner, sushi or pizza?” than “What’s your favorite dish?”

<div class="ids__space M"></div>

## 2 — We learned to recognize faces before we started using names

Humanity has only been using names for the last 30,000 years. The ability to tell faces apart and recognize them formed back in the days of bare-bottomed apes, tens of thousands of years earlier.

We learned to see pictures before we learned to read. To store fat before we learned to count calories and work out.

Whether or not it helps you remember your new colleague’s name, start using this in your work.
