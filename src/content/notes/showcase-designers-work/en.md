Imagine an orange. Hold it in your hands. Think about it… Now put it aside — we’ll come back to it. Trust me.

Meanwhile, about “pretty pictures”. Whatever you think of them, they matter.

They make a person want to dive into the meaning. It’s easier to work with admiration than to persuade, to beg someone to pay attention to weak work and promise that if they spend the time and try to figure it out, they’ll honestly, truly feel good.

Pick our orange up again. I hope you didn’t lose it. If you did, imagine it again.

The orange is design. The peel, the flesh and the seeds are the layers of our work.

Look: we choose an orange by its peel. How bright it is, how it plays with our senses, whether its smell makes us want to eat it or we feel something else. When we walk up to the counter, there are dozens of oranges in front of us, but we’ll only buy a couple.

The same thing happens when we get stuck in someone’s portfolio. There are dozens of projects in front of us, but we’ll open only a couple of links.

Once the orange has seduced us, we taste the flesh. Behind a beautiful peel there may be a dry, tasteless fruit, and we’ll be disappointed. Or it may be just as juicy as the peel promised, and we’ll be happy.

But an orange also has seeds.

What will a person get to in the end? What will we leave them to remember us by, once the orange is gone?
