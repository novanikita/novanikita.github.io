title: "The main secret of productivity"
meta:

---

Everyone has their own internal battery. If you usually get enough sleep and eat sensibly, it’s fairly predictable.

All you need for productivity is to know how many percent:

- you have when you wake up
- you need to fall asleep
- different tasks take

Everything else is simple math and planning.

That’s why other people’s planning systems don’t work: they were made by people with a different battery capacity. You also can’t increase your energy much. You can only raise it a tiny bit or make better use of what you have.

My battery is several thousand percent. A fuckton, really. I know that if I sleep well, drink tea in the morning, pet the cat, read at least a little and figure out what exactly I want to do today, I can work productively for hours on end. Then go for a run. And be happy. Because I know that if there’s more than 15–20% left in the battery, I just won’t fall asleep until I drain it enough.

I think any battery capacity is OK — 10%, 50%, 666%. What’s not OK is the battery changing and losing power. If half a year ago you had 500% and now you can barely scrape together 60%, it’s a reason to think about what happened.

The hardest part is understanding how much each task takes, and how circumstances can eat up more percent or, on the contrary, make the work more efficient.
